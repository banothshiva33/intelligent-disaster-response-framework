Title:An Intelligent Disaster Response Framework with Multi-Source Incident Verification and Machine Learning-Based Volunteer Allocation
Abstract
Natural disasters such as floods, earthquakes, cyclones, landslides, industrial accidents, and urban emergencies require rapid and coordinated response mechanisms to minimize the loss of human lives and infrastructure. Existing disaster management systems primarily focus on disaster reporting, communication, geographical information systems (GIS), and resource management. However, most of these systems assume that every reported incident is genuine and provide limited support for intelligent volunteer allocation. As a result, false disaster reports can lead to unnecessary deployment of emergency resources, delayed assistance to genuine victims, and inefficient utilization of available volunteers.
This project proposes An Intelligent Disaster Response Framework with Multi-Source Incident Verification and Machine Learning-Based Volunteer Allocation, a comprehensive disaster response platform designed to improve the reliability, efficiency, and intelligence of emergency management operations.
The proposed framework introduces a Multi-Source Disaster Verification Framework (MDVF) that validates every reported disaster before emergency resources are dispatched. Unlike conventional disaster management systems, every incident report must include mandatory evidence in the form of an image or video. The submitted report is then verified using three independent verification channels: existing platform users, nearby local users, and nearby registered volunteers. If strong confirmation is received from any one verification channel, the incident is immediately classified as verified, enabling rapid volunteer deployment without waiting for additional confirmations. If no confirmation is received within a predefined verification window, the system initiates a verification call through an emergency coordinator to validate the incident before allocation.
To further improve emergency response efficiency, the framework incorporates a Machine Learning-Based Disaster Severity Classification Module that predicts disaster severity, priority level, required volunteer skill categories, and estimated volunteer requirements using structured disaster information. Multiple machine learning algorithms, including Random Forest and XGBoost, will be evaluated to identify the most suitable classification model.
The proposed system also introduces a Smart Volunteer Allocation Engine that automatically ranks volunteers based on three weighted criteria: Skill Match (50%), Distance from the disaster location (30%), and Current Availability (20%). This multi-criteria ranking mechanism ensures that the most suitable and nearest available volunteers are assigned to verified incidents, significantly reducing response time while improving resource utilization.
To discourage misuse of the platform, the framework maintains complete audit logs, flags accounts associated with verified false reports, and updates user trust scores as a secondary credibility measure. Real-time notifications are delivered through Firebase Cloud Messaging, while an interactive GIS dashboard enables disaster management authorities to monitor verified incidents, volunteer locations, assignments, and emergency statistics.
The proposed framework aims to improve disaster verification accuracy, reduce emergency response delays, optimize volunteer allocation, minimize false incident reporting, and provide a scalable architecture suitable for deployment in real-world disaster management environments. The research contributes a practical, verification-driven disaster response framework that combines intelligent incident validation with machine learning-based volunteer allocation to enhance emergency response effectiveness.
Problem Statement
Disaster management is one of the most critical responsibilities of governments, humanitarian organizations, and emergency response agencies. During natural disasters and emergency situations, rapid decision-making and efficient coordination among responders directly influence the number of lives that can be saved. Although numerous disaster management systems have been proposed in recent years, most existing solutions primarily emphasize disaster reporting, communication, resource management, and geographical visualization.
A significant limitation of existing systems is the absence of a reliable mechanism to verify whether a citizen-submitted disaster report is genuine before emergency resources are mobilized. Current systems generally assume that every reported incident is authentic, making them vulnerable to false reporting, malicious misuse, duplicate reports, and accidental misinformation. Such false reports can consume valuable emergency resources, increase operational costs, delay assistance to genuine victims, and reduce public trust in disaster management platforms.
Furthermore, existing systems provide limited support for intelligent volunteer allocation. Volunteers are often assigned without considering disaster-specific skill requirements, geographical proximity, and current availability, resulting in inefficient deployment and slower response times.
Therefore, there is a need for an intelligent disaster response framework capable of verifying disaster authenticity using multiple independent verification sources while simultaneously allocating the most suitable volunteers through machine learning-assisted decision making. The proposed framework addresses these challenges by integrating multi-source incident verification, disaster severity assessment, and multi-criteria volunteer allocation into a unified emergency response platform.

Methodology
The proposed research follows a systematic eight-phase methodology covering data preparation, disaster verification, machine learning model development, volunteer allocation, application development, and experimental evaluation.
Phase 1: Disaster Dataset Collection and Preprocessing
The first phase focuses on collecting and preparing disaster-related datasets required for machine learning model development.
Activities include:
Collection of publicly available disaster datasets.
Data cleaning and preprocessing.
Handling missing values.
Data normalization.
Removal of duplicate records.
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).
Feature engineering for severity prediction.
The processed dataset will be divided into training, validation, and testing sets to ensure reliable model evaluation.
Phase 2: Machine Learning-Based Disaster Severity Classification
This phase develops a disaster severity prediction model capable of estimating the seriousness of a reported incident.
Input Features:
Disaster Type
Number of People Affected
Damage Level
Location Category
Disaster Description Features
Predicted Outputs:
Severity Level
Priority Level
Required Skill Categories
Estimated Volunteer Requirement
The following machine learning algorithms will be implemented and compared:
Random Forest
XGBoost
Model evaluation will be performed using standard classification metrics to identify the best-performing model.
Phase 3: Multi-Source Disaster Verification Framework (MDVF)
This phase introduces the primary research contribution of the proposed framework.
Stage 1 – Evidence Validation
Every disaster report must include:
Image or
Video
Reports submitted without supporting evidence are rejected before entering the verification workflow.
Stage 2 – Multi-Source Verification
The submitted report is simultaneously forwarded to three independent verification channels:
Existing Platform Users
Nearby Local Users
Nearby Registered Volunteers
Each verification source can respond with:
Confirm
Reject
Unable to Verify
Stage 3 – Strong Confirmation Decision
If strong confirmation is received from any one trusted verification channel, the disaster report is immediately classified as Verified, and the volunteer allocation process begins without waiting for responses from the remaining verification sources.
This significantly reduces emergency response delays while maintaining report authenticity.
Stage 4 – Verification Call
If no verification response is received within the predefined verification period:
The system initiates a verification request.
An emergency coordinator contacts the reporting user.
Additional information is collected.
The coordinator approves or rejects the incident.
Stage 5 – False Report Handling
If a report is confirmed to be intentionally false:
User account is flagged.
Trust score is reduced.
Complete audit logs are maintained.
Future reports from the same account receive additional scrutiny.
Phase 4: Smart Volunteer Allocation Engine
Once a disaster report is verified, the Smart Volunteer Allocation Engine identifies the most suitable responders.
The volunteer allocation score is calculated using three weighted parameters:
Skill Match – 50%
Distance – 30%
Availability – 20%
Each registered volunteer is evaluated using these parameters, and volunteers are ranked in descending order of their final score.
The highest-ranked volunteers are selected for deployment.
Distance calculations are performed using the Haversine Formula to accurately determine geographical proximity between volunteers and disaster locations.
Phase 5: Notification and Assignment Module
Following volunteer selection:
Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) is used to notify assigned volunteers.
Volunteers receive complete incident information, including:
Disaster Type
Severity Level
Location
Required Skills
Reporting Time
The system also tracks volunteer acknowledgment and assignment status.
Phase 6: Administrative Dashboard and GIS Monitoring
A web-based administrative dashboard is developed for disaster management authorities.
The dashboard provides:
Live Disaster Monitoring
Verified Incident List
Volunteer Availability Status
Volunteer Assignment Tracking
Disaster Verification Statistics
Emergency Response Analytics
GIS-Based Disaster Visualization
Interactive maps developed using Leaflet.js enable authorities to monitor disaster locations and volunteer distribution in real time.
Phase 7: Experimental Evaluation
The proposed framework will be evaluated against multiple baseline approaches, including:
Random Volunteer Allocation
Skill-Based Allocation
Distance-Based Allocation
Existing IEEE Base Paper Approach
The evaluation focuses on determining improvements in disaster verification accuracy, volunteer allocation effectiveness, and emergency response efficiency.
Phase 8: Comparative Analysis
The final phase compares the proposed framework with the IEEE base paper by analyzing improvements in:
Disaster Report Verification
Volunteer Allocation Intelligence
Response Time
Volunteer Utilization
Resource Optimization
Overall Emergency Response Performance
The results will demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating multi-source disaster verification with machine learning-based volunteer allocation in real-world disaster management scenarios.

The primary contribution of this research is the design and implementation of a Multi-Source Disaster Verification Framework (MDVF) for validating disaster reports before emergency resources are deployed.
Unlike existing disaster management systems, which assume that every reported incident is genuine, the proposed framework introduces a structured verification mechanism that authenticates disaster reports using multiple independent verification sources.
The framework performs disaster verification through:

This verification process minimizes false disaster reporting while ensuring that genuine incidents receive immediate emergency response.
Contribution 2: Mandatory Evidence-Based Incident Reporting
The proposed framework introduces mandatory multimedia evidence submission during disaster reporting.

Video

Evidence validation becomes the first layer of disaster authentication before any emergency resource allocation is initiated.
Contribution 3: Strong Confirmation-Based Incident Verification
The proposed framework introduces a rapid verification strategy based on strong confirmation.
Unlike conventional systems that wait for multiple verification responses, the proposed framework immediately classifies a disaster report as Verified when strong confirmation is received from any one trusted verification source.

The system automatically initiates a verification request, allowing an emergency coordinator to contact the reporting user and collect additional information before approving or rejecting the incident.
This fallback mechanism reduces false negatives while maintaining emergency response reliability.

Measures the percentage of disaster reports correctly classified as genuine or false.

Verification Accuracy =(Number of Correctly Verified Reports / Total Verified Reports) × 100
2. Average Verification Time
Measures the average time required to verify a disaster report.
A lower verification time indicates a more responsive disaster verification framework.
3. Severity Classification Accuracy
Evaluates the accuracy of the machine learning model in predicting disaster severity.
The following metrics will be used:
Accuracy
Precision
Recall
F1-Score
4. Volunteer Allocation Accuracy
Measures how effectively the system assigns volunteers possessing the required skills for each disaster.
This metric evaluates the correctness of volunteer matching.
5. Average Response Time
Measures the total time from disaster report submission to volunteer assignment.
Lower response time indicates better emergency response efficiency.
6. Volunteer Utilization Rate
Measures the percentage of available volunteers successfully utilized during disaster response.
Higher utilization indicates improved resource management.
7. False Report Detection Rate
Measures the ability of the proposed framework to identify intentionally false disaster reports.
This metric evaluates the effectiveness of the Multi-Source Disaster Verification Framework.
8. System Throughput
Measures the number of disaster reports processed successfully within a given period.
This metric evaluates the scalability of the proposed framework.

Conclusion
The proposed research introduces a comprehensive disaster response framework that extends existing disaster management systems by integrating disaster authenticity verification with intelligent volunteer allocation. Through the Multi-Source Disaster Verification Framework, mandatory evidence validation, machine learning-based disaster severity assessment, and a multi-criteria volunteer allocation engine, the proposed system aims to improve emergency response reliability, reduce false disaster reporting, and optimize volunteer deployment.
Unlike conventional disaster management platforms, the proposed framework ensures that emergency resources are allocated only after systematic verification while maintaining rapid response through strong confirmation-based decision making. The proposed framework not only addresses the limitations identified in the IEEE base paper but also establishes a strong foundation for future research in intelligent, trustworthy, and data-driven disaster response systems.
